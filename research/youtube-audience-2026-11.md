## 4. Lessons for Echo

These are inferences from the public figures above, not YouTube Studio analytics.

1. **Put the contradiction in the title, not after the intro.** The two dated competitor hits lead with refusal or erasure (“The #1 Song MTV Refused to Play,” “The Underground Railroad hero you've never heard of”). Echo’s own best history titles do the same (“She Walked Alone,” “The Black Acting Governor Arkansas Forgot,” “Born Enslaved. Died the Richest Black Man in Arkansas”). Soft or commemorative titles underperform those contrast titles on this channel.

2. **Name one person and one concrete fact.** Competitor and Echo leaders are not “a chapter of Black history.” They are Eckford walking alone, Jerry Jewell forgotten as acting governor, Lewis Hayden, Daisy Bates’s house as a war room. Keep that specificity.

3. **Hometown memory is the comment engine Echo actually has.** In a sample this small, the only organic comments were pride, “I saw him,” and condolence on living or recently deceased Little Rock figures. The 1,000-view Eckford Short still had no viewer comments — only Echo’s subscribe CTA. Closes that invite “I remember” or “my people came through [town]” (Wiley already does this for Pine Bluff) fit the evidence better than a generic “like and subscribe.”

4. **Do not ask a question the story cannot support.** The one critical comment in the sample pushed back because a county judge was asked what he should do “on his first day,” as if he had been elected mayor or president. End on a true, precise beat.

5. **Length: 60–180 seconds is the competitive band for a sourced story Short.** Confirmed recent competitor hits ran **1:40** and **2:58**. Echo’s Wiley (1:08) and Daisy (1:10) sit in that band. Charlotte at **33 seconds** is shorter than both dated competitor examples. This is not proof that 33 seconds cannot work; it is evidence that story Shorts in this niche are allowed to run long, even to the three-minute cap, if the first line holds.

6. **Local and timely still outperform evergreen biography on this channel, but contrast-history can travel.** Geese Ausbie (669), Wendell Griffen (614), the restaurant award (668), UAPB homecoming (579), and the HBCU parking story (356) show that. Wiley’s 325 views and 23 likes, shortly after posting, show that a sharp historical contrast can move too. Do not abandon sourced history; sharpen the hook and keep the Arkansas place-name in the title.

7. **Do not chase national celebrity-history volume.** Unfiltered’s 1.8 million views on a Michael Jackson/MTV Short is a different business (452,000 subscribers, entertainment category, almost three minutes, thousands of comments). Echo’s lane is documented Arkansas history. The transferable part is the hook style and length, not the subject matter. Use Arkansas music and culture figures when they are true and unused — not Motown catalog drama.

## 5. Topic ideas these signals support, and Echo has not already filed

Excluded: Wiley Jones, Charlotte Stephens, Daisy Bates, Ulysses Baugh, and every subject in issues **#1–26** and in `ideas/STORY-IDEAS.md` (including T11–T15 and the additional vetted list). Also excluded: people and stories already titled on the Echo channel (Elizabeth Eckford, Jerry Jewell, Pharoah Sanders, Geese Ausbie, Dunbar, Lottie Shackelford, Charles Bussey, Ninth Street / Billie Holiday, and others).

These are research leads from YouTube interest signals, not finished scripts. Open primary sources before writing a voiceover. Do not invent a face or use cotton-field imagery.

1. **The Green Book in Arkansas.** A THV11 segment, “Here are some historical sites in Arkansas with ties to the Green Book” (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9lhsyQghY2k, **136 views**, published about 8 months before this pull, 2:14), shows the topic exists and is under-covered. It fits Echo’s place-based lane and the “how did people actually travel” hook that already travels in adjacent Black history content. Not in the issue list.

2. **Free Black Arkansans before the Civil War.** Search returned “The Hidden History of Free Black Arkansas” (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ILn0-P4rLI, **125 views**, about 2 years old, 1:01). That is a “you’ve never heard of” gap in-state, in the same pattern as the 368,836-view Hayden Short, without repeating Bass Reeves or Wiley Jones. Needs Encyclopedia of Arkansas and county records before any script.

3. **The “other” Great Migration — African Americans moving into Arkansas, 1865–1920.** The Arkansas State Archives lecture by Dr. Story Matkin-Rawn (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5CZTSnqXSMk) has **11,629 views**; a related Archives lecture by Dr. Kenneth Barnes on African migration to Arkansas in the late 1800s (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XzhLrKXRB-I) has **14,371 views**. That is real interest in a state-level story Echo has not filed. Keep it a documented movement story, not a claim about any one family (issue #6 already covers Thanksgiving genealogy how-tos).

4. **Anderson L. Rush, Reconstruction official.** The Archives posted “Rush to Freedom: the Story of Anderson L. Rush” about one month before this pull (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EqCKbx5PK68, **272 views**, 49:34). Archer Shepperson (#4) is already filed; Rush is a different official in the same timely Reconstruction series. A Short could do what the 49-minute lecture cannot.

5. **The Arkansas Farm Bureau’s “Negro Division.”** An existing 50-second Short by R.A. Howard, “The Forgotten Story of the Arkansas Farm Bureau's 'Negro Division'” (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EoRZ15Dp1Nw, **1,099 views**), shows the hook already works at Short length. Echo has not filed it. If pursued, tell the institutional story accurately and do **not** illustrate it with cotton-field imagery.
