Wiley Jones

Born enslaved. Died the richest Black man in Arkansas. And the streetcars in Pine Bluff? Those were his.

Walter Wiley Jones was born in Georgia in 1848, brought to Jefferson County as a child, and built a mule-drawn streetcar line in Pine Bluff. A city book of 1893 put his fortune at $200,000. He died December 7, 1904.

Charlotte Andrews Stephens

She was fifteen — and made Little Rock history.

In 1869 she became the city’s first Black public-school teacher. Her career spanned seventy years. Little Rock named Stephens Elementary for her in 1910. She retired in 1939.

Daisy Lee Gatson Bates

Daisy Bates was born on Veterans Day. Her house was the war room for the Little Rock Nine.

By most records she was born November 11th, 1914, in Huttig, Arkansas. She and L.C. Bates published the Arkansas State Press. When nine Black teenagers set out to integrate Central High, they started the day at her house — and they ended it there. The house at 1207 West 28th Street is a National Historic Landmark. No licensed portrait of Daisy Bates was available; this short does not invent her face.
