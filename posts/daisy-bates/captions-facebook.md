Daisy Bates was born on Veterans Day. In 1957, her house in Little Rock was the war room for the Little Rock Nine.

By most records, Daisy Lee Gatson Bates was born November 11th, 1914, in Huttig, Arkansas. She and her husband published the Arkansas State Press, and she led the state NAACP. When nine Black teenagers set out to integrate Central High, they started the day at her house — and they ended it there. A rock came through the window with a note: “Stone this time. Dynamite next.” On September 25th, 1957, after President Eisenhower sent the 101st Airborne, the Nine walked into Central High under Army escort, picked up at her door.

That house is a National Historic Landmark. This year Arkansas put a new marker there. The command post is still standing.

Did your Arkansas history class tell you the Little Rock Nine had a war room — and a woman running it?
