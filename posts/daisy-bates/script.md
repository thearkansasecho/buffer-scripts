# Daisy Bates — narration script

Spoken on the review preview. Punctuation is for reading and the record;
word-level timings from the finished encode are not stored in this repo
(large media stays on the box, not committed).

Daisy Bates was born on Veterans Day. In 1957, her house in Little Rock
was the war room for the Little Rock Nine.

By most records, Daisy Lee Gatson Bates was born November 11th, 1914,
in Huttig, Arkansas. She and her husband, L.C., published the Arkansas
State Press. She led the state NAACP. When nine Black teenagers set out
to integrate Central High, they started the day at her house — and they
ended it there.

Segregationists knew that. A rock came through the living-room window
with a note: Stone this time. Dynamite next. Crosses burned in the yard.
Shots were fired from a passing car. The house became a fortress.

On September 25th, 1957, after President Eisenhower sent the 101st
Airborne, those nine students walked into Central High under Army escort.
They had been picked up at Daisy Bates's door.

That house is a National Historic Landmark. This year, Arkansas put a
new marker there. The command post is still standing.

Did your Arkansas history class tell you the Little Rock Nine had a war
room — and a woman running it?

This is The Arkansas Echo.
