# Arkansas bar exam score-tampering case — narration script

Spoken on the review preview. Punctuation is for reading and the record;
word-level timings from the finished encode are stored on the box
(`/workspace/echo-bar-exam/bar-word-timings.json`), not in this repo
(large media stays on the box, not committed).

This is a pending criminal case. The script treats every allegation as
an allegation. Nancie Givens has pleaded not guilty and is presumed
innocent.

She ran the office that decides who becomes a lawyer in Arkansas.
Prosecutors say she took points off some bar exams and put them on
others — for five years. She has pleaded not guilty.

Nancie Givens led the Arkansas Supreme Court's Office of Professional
Programs. On November 5th, 2026, she was charged with sixty-five felony
counts of tampering with a public record. She is presumed innocent.

Six justices say the alleged changes ran across at least eight bar
exams, from July 2020 to July 2025. The passing score is two hundred
seventy. They wrote that evidence exists she harvested points from some
examinees and added them to others, pushing some scores over the passing
line.

In at least one case, they say, someone who would have passed was told
they failed. In another, the real top scorer had points taken away, and
someone else got the honor. Arresting documents, reported in the press,
identified at least sixty-four people whose scores were altered.

The justices also said about eleven thousand four hundred files were
deleted or made unavailable in early January — records the office was
required to keep for twenty-five years. Givens resigned in February. An
internal review found the irregularities, and the FBI and State Police
opened a probe in April.

If these allegations are true, the justices wrote, this is an
unprecedented breach of public trust. They said the vast majority of
people told they passed did, in fact, pass. Those who were affected will
be notified once their status is confirmed.

Did you, or someone you know, take the Arkansas bar between 2020 and
2025?

This is The Arkansas Echo.
