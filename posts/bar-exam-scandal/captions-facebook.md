She ran the office that decides who becomes a lawyer in Arkansas. Prosecutors say she took points off some bar exams and put them on others — for five years. She has pleaded not guilty.

Nancie Givens, the former executive director of the Arkansas Supreme Court's Office of Professional Programs, was charged on November 5, 2026, with 65 felony counts of tampering with a public record. She is presumed innocent.

Six justices of the court say the alleged changes ran across at least eight bar exams, from July 2020 to July 2025. The passing score is 270. They wrote that evidence exists she harvested points from some examinees and added them to others, pushing some scores over the passing line. In at least one case, they say, someone who would have passed was told they failed. In another, the real top scorer had points taken away, and someone else got the honor. Arresting documents, reported in the press, identified at least 64 people whose scores were altered.

The justices also said about 11,400 files were deleted or made unavailable in early January 2026 — records the office was required to keep for 25 years. Givens resigned in February. An internal review found the irregularities, and the FBI and Arkansas State Police opened a probe in April.

If these allegations are true, the justices wrote, this is an unprecedented breach of public trust. They said the vast majority of people told they passed did, in fact, pass. Those who were affected will be notified once their status is confirmed.
