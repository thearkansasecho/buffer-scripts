**Title:** Arkansas Ran Him Out for Being Free. He Came Back a Minister.

**Description:**

They brought Nathan Warren to Arkansas enslaved. Then the state ran him out for being free. He came back as a minister.

In 1819, Robert Crittenden, the first secretary of the Arkansas Territory, brought him here enslaved. Warren later lived in Little Rock as a free man — known as Little Rock's Confectioner. Then white agitators pushed a ban on free Black residents. In 1859, Act 151 ordered free African Americans out of Arkansas, or be sold into slavery for a year. Warren had to leave.

He returned during the Civil War as a minister, and led the state's first A.M.E. congregation. Bethel A.M.E. in Little Rock grew from that work. So did Bethel Institute, the school we now call Shorter College.

On November 19th, 1868 — one hundred fifty-eight years ago next week — Arkansas held its first African Methodist Episcopal Annual Conference, under Bishop James A. Shorter. By 1890, the church had three hundred thirty-three congregations in this state, and nearly twenty-eight thousand members.
