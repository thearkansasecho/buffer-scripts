# Wiley Jones — sources and licenses

Every still used on screen is a faithful 2D reproduction of a work
**published in the United States before 1930** (1887, 1889, or 1893) and is
in the **public domain**. No Encyclopedia of Arkansas or Butler Center
“courtesy of” photographs were used. No AI faces. No invented portraits
presented as photographs. No cotton imagery (body text about cotton or the
“Cotton Belt” railroad was kept off screen). Not labeled 4K; native
1080×1920 render.

   Biographical and Historical Memoirs, 1889. Published 1889. Public domain.”

   “William J. Simmons, Men of Mark, 1887, pp. 278–280.”

   composite engraving, 1893 book. Public domain. Credit uses the plate’s
   own caption.
4. **Pine Bluff street engravings**, 1893 World’s Fair Edition. Main Street
   and Pine Street. Jones owned the city’s consolidated street-railway
   lines in 1893 (primary text, p. 26 and p. 38); the credit does **not**
   claim a given engraving is captioned as “his car.” Body text on those
   pages that discusses cotton wagons or acreage was cropped out. Public

5. **1893 World’s Fair Edition cover:** Jefferson County courthouse
   engraving, with a streetcar in the foreground. Public domain. Used for

6. **Jefferson County map** from the same 1893 book. Public domain. Used
   when the narration says he was brought to Jefferson County.

The 1893 p. 26 fortune page and p. 38 property-text page were researched
and **not** used on screen: the former includes a house engraving not
identified as his; the latter mixes other people’s copy and a
“Cotton Belt R. R.” mention.

## Fact sources for the narration

- **“Born enslaved.”** Goodspeed 1889, p. 801: mother Ann was “one of
  George Jones’ slaves”; the family was sold after his death in 1858.
  *Men of Mark* 1887, p. 278, describes an “uneducated slave-boy.”
  Encyclopedia of Arkansas (EOA) entry “Wiley Jones (1848–1904),” last
  updated April 22, 2026:
  https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/wiley-jones-337/
- **“Died the richest Black man in Arkansas.”** EOA: “He was the richest
  Black person in the state at the time of his death.” Independently:
  John Kirk, “Walter ‘Wiley’ Jones (1848-1904),” UA Little Rock Public
  Radio / Arkansas Moment, January 31, 2016,
  https://www.ualrpublicradio.org/2016-01-31/walter-wiley-jones-1848-1904
  The EOA figure of a $300,000 estate at death is **not** used in the
  voiceover (single-source dollar amount).
- **“And the streetcars in Pine Bluff? Those were his.”** *Men of Mark*
  1887, p. 280 (charter and first car). 1893 World’s Fair Edition, p. 26:
  “The two street car lines consolidated, of Pine Bluff, with a handsome
  park and race course, are owned by Wiley Jones.” P. 38: “His Citizens’
  Street Railway, twelve miles in length…”
- **Walter Wiley Jones, born in Georgia in 1848, brought to Jefferson
  County as a child.** Goodspeed p. 801 and *Men of Mark* p. 279: born
  Madison County, Georgia, July 14, 1848; taken to Arkansas at age five.
  The voiceover uses the conservative “Georgia in 1848” / “as a child,”
  not the exact day.
- **“His father, a white planter, said he’d left papers to free the
  family. The papers were never found. In 1858, they were sold.”**
  Goodspeed p. 801: George Jones, a Georgia planter, on his death-bed in
  1858 told his wife he had made provision for their freedom; “no
  manumission papers could be found”; the administrator sold the family.
  EOA agrees. *Men of Mark* does not tell this episode and is not the
  source for it.
- **Barber, waited tables for meals, saved, loaned money at interest.**
  Goodspeed p. 802; *Men of Mark* p. 279 independently has him at the
  barber’s chair from 1868 to 1881.
- **Real estate, a mercantile, a fifty-five-acre park with a racetrack.**
  *Men of Mark* p. 280; Goodspeed p. 802; 1893 p. 38 describes the Pine
  Grove Stock Farm of fifty-five acres (and a separate eight-acre
  Citizens’ Park). The voiceover follows the 1887–89 “fifty-five-acre
  park with a racetrack” wording.
- **Charter August 1886; first car October 19.** *Men of Mark* p. 280:
  charter “August, 1886”; “ran the first car on October 19, 1886.” EOA:
  mule-drawn line, August 1886. The voiceover says “mule-drawn streetcar
  line” and does **not** say “the first” or “the only in the world”
  (Goodspeed’s promotional “only colored owner of a street railway in
  the world” was rejected).
- **By 1893, about twelve miles and a fortune of two hundred thousand
  dollars.** 1893 World’s Fair Edition, p. 38, “twelve miles in length”
  (a railways note also gives 12½ miles — “about twelve” covers both);
  p. 26: “who has made a fortune of $200,000 within the past twenty
  years.” Company capitalization of $200,000 is not used as the
  personal-fortune figure.
- **“He never married.”** *Men of Mark* p. 280 (1887); Goodspeed p. 802
  (1889). EOA treats him as unmarried and describes an estate settled
  because he left no will, with no widow named. No contradictory
  evidence was found. The completed-fact wording is the standard
  secondary account; the two contemporaneous books only attest it
  through 1889.
- **Died December 7, 1904.** EOA (heart attack and Bright’s disease, at
  home in Pine Bluff). Kirk / UA Little Rock Public Radio, as above.

## Facts researched and intentionally omitted

- Circuit clerk of Jefferson County, 1892–1894 (EOA only in our check).
- Estate “worth more than $300,000” at death (EOA; the voiceover uses
  the 1893 primary $200,000 plus “richest”).
- “Left no will” (EOA only).
- “Only colored owner of a street railway in the world” (Goodspeed,
  promotional).
- “One of the first African Americans in the nation to receive a
  franchise” (EOA).
- Confederate camp servant for Col. Fountain Pitts Yell (EOA /
  Goodspeed). *Men of Mark* says he “followed his master into the
  Federal army,” which conflicts; the whole army episode was left out.
- Saloon: EOA “opened a successful saloon” in 1876 vs. Goodspeed
  “hired at a saloon, remaining one year.” Tension; omitted.
- Pine Bluff Transit as a later municipal descendant (EOA “forerunner”
  language).
- Cotton work, cotton wagons, cotton gin (Goodspeed / *Men of Mark*).
  Omitted, and no cotton imagery.
