# Video B — Sgt. Ulysses Grant Baugh Sr. / Mosaic Templars “A Soldier’s Story”

**Not built.** Preview was withheld because no usable licensed or public-domain photograph of the bowl, the exhibit, or Sgt. Baugh was found. News and museum-courtesy photographs are not a license to post.

## What the sources actually say

Primary story: Nathan Treece, “Mosaic Templars Cultural Center opens ‘A Soldier’s Story,’” UA Little Rock Public Radio (KUAR), November 10, 2026.
https://www.ualrpublicradio.org/local-regional-news/2026-11-10/mosaic-templars-cultural-center-opens-a-soldiers-story

Supporting museum press write-up: “Mosaic Templars Cultural Center announces opening reception for ‘A Soldier’s Story’ exhibition,” *Stuttgart Daily Leader*, October 29, 2026 (source line: Mosaic Templars Cultural Center).
https://www.stuttgartdailyleader.com/mosaic-templars-cultural-center-announces-opening-reception-for-a-soldiers-story-exhibition/

Verified or well-supported claims:

- The exhibit *A Soldier’s Story* (full title in KUAR closing: “A Soldier’s Story: The Legacy of African American Service Members”) opened Tuesday, November 10, 2026, at the Mosaic Templars Cultural Center in downtown Little Rock. It is free and open to the public.
- KUAR says it runs through **March 20, 2027**. The museum press write-up says through **March 27, 2027**. Do not pick one date in a voiceover until the museum confirms it.
- Community-sourced artifacts, including objects loaned by families of Arkansas veterans.
- Sgt. Ulysses Grant Baugh (KUAR: “Sr.”; press write-up: of **College Station**) served in the Korean War, was held as a POW, and the bowl he carried is in the exhibit.
- Press write-up (museum-sourced): U.S. Army, 9th Infantry Regiment, 2nd Infantry Division; captured **November 25, 1950**; held nearly three years at **Camp 5 in Pyoktong, North Korea**, described as notorious, with extreme conditions and a high death toll.
- KUAR caption: the bowl “was a lifeline for food and drink in a Korean POW camp. He also used it as an anchor to stay grounded throughout his imprisonment.”
- Executive Director Key Fletcher (KUAR): “that bowl for Sgt. Baugh really represented his life.” He “survived being a POW in the Korean War, and he came back and didn’t share that story a whole lot.” The family lent the object.
- Press write-up: “Used for both food and water, the bowl became far more than a utensil – it was his lifeline, a source of hope that he would survive.” After returning home he rarely spoke of the ordeal, protected the bowl, and later entrusted it to his daughter, **Sonja Baugh McDaniel**, with the instruction **“Protect it.”**

## The rejected hook

The draft hook “that bowl is why he made it out of a Korean prison camp” is **not supported**. Sources call the bowl a lifeline for food and water, an anchor to stay grounded, a representation of his life, and a source of hope that he would survive. They do not say the bowl caused his survival. A truthful hook, if the film is made later:

> “A Black Arkansas soldier’s food bowl is in a museum this Veterans Day. In a Korean prison camp, that bowl was his lifeline.”

Or, staying closer to Fletcher:

> “They put a Black Arkansas POW’s food bowl in a museum. He said little about the camp after he got home. The bowl still tells it.”

## Uncertainties — do not voice these without another source

- **Rank:** The exhibit and KUAR say **Sergeant**. A Hall of Valor / NARA repatriated-POW database entry for Corporal Ulysses G. Baugh, ASN RA-18294268, Arkansas County, born January 17, 1931, captured November 25, 1950, released August 7, 1953, likely refers to the same man (the capture date matches), but the rank and home county differ (Arkansas County vs. College Station in Pulaski County). He may have been promoted later, or the database may be incomplete. Use the museum’s “Sgt.” only while noting the database, or confirm with the family / museum before locking a script.
- **Release date August 7, 1953** and “nearly three years” are consistent with each other; the exact release day is from the Hall of Valor / NARA database, not from KUAR. Prefer “nearly three years” unless the museum or NARA record is cited on screen.
- Closing date of the exhibit: March 20 vs. March 27, 2027.

## Why there is no video yet

The only photographs of the bowl found in this pass are:

- Nathan Treece / Little Rock Public Radio photographs in the KUAR story (news photographs, not licensed to Echo).
- “Korean War bowl owned by Ulysses Grant Baugh. (Photo courtesy of the Mosaic Templars Cultural Center.)” in the *Stuttgart Daily Leader* press write-up (museum courtesy; no license to Echo).

No Library of Congress, National Archives, or other public-domain portrait of Baugh was located. Official Korean War personnel photographs were not found under his name in time for this pass. Using another POW’s official Army photograph as if it were Baugh would be dishonest.
